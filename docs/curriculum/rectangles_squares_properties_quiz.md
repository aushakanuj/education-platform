# Properties of Rectangles and Squares Quiz

## Multiple-Choice Questions

1. **[Easy]** Which of the following is the precise mathematical definition of a rectangle?
   A) A quadrilateral with four equal sides.
   B) A quadrilateral in which the angles are all $90^\circ$.
   C) A quadrilateral with diagonals intersecting at $45^\circ$.
   D) A quadrilateral with only one pair of parallel sides.

2. **[Easy]** What is the measure of the angle formed where the diagonals of a square intersect?
   A) $45^\circ$
   B) $60^\circ$
   C) $90^\circ$
   D) $180^\circ$

3. **[Easy]** If one diagonal of a rectangle is $8\text{ cm}$ long, what must be the length of the other diagonal?
   A) $4\text{ cm}$
   B) $8\text{ cm}$
   C) $16\text{ cm}$
   D) It cannot be determined without knowing the width.

4. **[Easy]** Which statement about squares and rectangles is ALWAYS true?
   A) Every rectangle is a square.
   B) Every square is a rectangle.
   C) No rectangle is a square.
   D) No square is a rectangle.

5. **[Medium]** In rectangle $ABCD$, the diagonals intersect at point $O$. If the segment $OA = 5\text{ cm}$, what is the length of diagonal $BD$?
   A) $5\text{ cm}$
   B) $10\text{ cm}$
   C) $15\text{ cm}$
   D) $20\text{ cm}$

6. **[Medium]** In square $ABCD$, diagonal $AC$ is drawn. What is the measure of $\angle CAB$?
   A) $30^\circ$
   B) $45^\circ$
   C) $60^\circ$
   D) $90^\circ$

7. **[Medium]** Which triangle congruence criterion is used to prove that the diagonals of a rectangle are equal in length?
   A) SSS (Side-Side-Side)
   B) ASA (Angle-Side-Angle)
   C) SAS (Side-Angle-Side)
   D) AAS (Angle-Angle-Side)

8. **[Medium]** In the Venn diagram of quadrilaterals, where does the set of squares lie?
   A) Entirely outside parallelograms
   B) In the intersection of rectangles and rhombuses
   C) In the intersection of trapeziums and kites, but outside parallelograms
   D) Entirely inside rhombuses but completely separate from rectangles

9. **[Hard]** Let $ABCD$ be a quadrilateral where the diagonals are equal and bisect each other. If the angle between the diagonals is $x$, which of the following is true?
   A) It is only a rectangle when $x = 90^\circ$.
   B) It is a rectangle for any value of $x$.
   C) It is a square for any value of $x$.
   D) It can never be a rectangle if $x \neq 60^\circ$.

10. **[Hard]** Suppose we have two sticks of equal length and a piece of thread. We tie the sticks together at their midpoints. When we stretch the thread around their endpoints, what shape is guaranteed to form, and what happens if the sticks are perpendicular?
    A) Always a square; a rectangle if perpendicular
    B) Always a rectangle; a square if perpendicular
    C) Always a rhombus; a square if perpendicular
    D) Always a parallelogram; a rectangle if perpendicular

## Answer Key

1. **B** — A rectangle is defined simply as a quadrilateral in which all angles are $90^\circ$. Opposite sides being equal is a property that follows from this.
2. **C** — The diagonals of a square are perpendicular bisectors, meaning they intersect at exactly $90^\circ$.
3. **B** — The diagonals of a rectangle always have the same length, so the second diagonal must also be $8\text{ cm}$ long.
4. **B** — A square is a special type of rectangle with equal sides, so every square is a rectangle, but not all rectangles have equal sides to be squares.
5. **B** — Since the diagonals of a rectangle bisect each other, if $OA = 5\text{ cm}$, then $AC = 10\text{ cm}$. Since diagonals are equal, $BD = AC = 10\text{ cm}$.
6. **B** — The diagonals of a square bisect the corner angles. Since the corner is $90^\circ$, diagonal $AC$ splits it into $45^\circ$.
7. **C** — We use SAS congruence ($\triangle ADC \cong \triangle DAB$) with equal opposite sides, a shared base, and the $90^\circ$ right angles between them.
8. **B** — A square has all $90^\circ$ angles (rectangle set) and all equal sides (rhombus set), placing it in the intersection of both sets inside parallelograms.
9. **B** — If diagonals are equal and bisect each other, the shape is always a rectangle, regardless of the angle $x$ between the diagonals.
10. **B** — Tying equal-length sticks at their midpoints ensures the diagonals are equal and bisect each other, forming a rectangle. If they are perpendicular, it satisfies the diagonal conditions for a square.
