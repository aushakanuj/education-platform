# Quadrilaterals and Their Properties

## Slide 1 — Welcome & Lesson Objectives
Welcome to our lesson on Quadrilaterals! In this lesson, we will explore:
- What defines a **quadrilateral** and its basic properties.
- The **Angle Sum Property** of any quadrilateral.
- Definitions and unique properties of special quadrilaterals:
  - **Rectangles and Squares**
  - **Parallelograms**
  - **Rhombuses**
  - **Kites and Trapeziums**
- How to classify these shapes using **Venn diagrams**.

## Slide 2 — Prerequisites: Angles and Triangles
Before we begin, remember these fundamental concepts:
- **Transversal Lines:** When a line cuts through two parallel lines, alternate interior angles are equal, and the sum of interior angles on the same side of the transversal is $180^\circ$.
- **Congruent Triangles:** Triangles that are identical in shape and size. We prove congruence using rules like SAS (Side-Angle-Side), AAS (Angle-Angle-Side), SSS (Side-Side-Side), or ASA (Angle-Side-Angle).
- **Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles (CPCT):** If two triangles are congruent, their corresponding sides and angles are equal.
- **Triangle Angle Sum:** The sum of angles in any triangle is $180^\circ$.

## Slide 3 — What is a Quadrilateral?
- **Definition:** The word "quadrilateral" comes from Latin: *quadri* meaning "four" and *latus* meaning "sides". It is a closed geometric figure with four straight sides.
- **Angles:** A quadrilateral has four interior angles formed between its sides.
- **Angle Sum Property:** The sum of all four interior angles in *any* quadrilateral is **$360^\circ$**.
  - *Visual Proof:* If we draw a diagonal in a quadrilateral, we divide it into two triangles. Since each triangle's angles sum to $180^\circ$, the quadrilateral's angles sum to $180^\circ + 180^\circ = 360^\circ$.

## Slide 4 — Rectangles
- **Definition:** A rectangle is a quadrilateral in which all four angles are right angles ($90^\circ$).
- **Key Properties:**
  1. **All angles are $90^\circ$**.
  2. **Opposite sides are equal** in length ($AB = CD$, $AD = BC$).
  3. **Opposite sides are parallel** to each other ($AB \parallel CD$, $AD \parallel BC$).
  4. **Diagonals are equal in length** ($AC = BD$).
  5. **Diagonals bisect each other** (intersect at their midpoints, so $OA = OC$ and $OB = OD$).

## Slide 5 — Squares: The Special Rectangle
- **Definition:** A square is a special type of rectangle where all four sides are of equal length.
- **Venn Diagram Connection:** Every square is a rectangle, but not every rectangle is a square!
- **Key Properties:**
  1. **All sides are equal** to each other.
  2. **Opposite sides are parallel**.
  3. **All angles are $90^\circ$**.
  4. **Diagonals are equal and bisect each other at $90^\circ$** (they are perpendicular bisectors).
  5. **Diagonals bisect the angles** of the square, dividing each $90^\circ$ angle into two equal $45^\circ$ halves.

## Slide 6 — Parallelograms
- **Definition:** A parallelogram is a quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel opposite sides.
- **Key Properties:**
  1. **Opposite sides are parallel** ($AB \parallel CD$, $AD \parallel BC$).
  2. **Opposite sides are equal** in length.
  3. **Opposite angles are equal** ($\angle A = \angle C$, $\angle B = \angle D$).
  4. **Adjacent angles add up to $180^\circ$** (supplementary interior angles).
  5. **Diagonals bisect each other** (but they are *not* necessarily equal in length).

## Slide 7 — Rhombuses
- **Definition:** A rhombus is a quadrilateral in which all four sides have the same length.
- **Parallelogram Connection:** Since its opposite sides are parallel, every rhombus is also a parallelogram, and inherits all parallelogram properties.
- **Key Properties:**
  1. **All sides are equal** in length.
  2. **Opposite sides are parallel**.
  3. **Opposite angles are equal**, and adjacent angles add up to $180^\circ$.
  4. **Diagonals bisect each other at right angles ($90^\circ$)**.
  5. **Diagonals bisect the angles** of the rhombus.

## Slide 8 — Kites
- **Definition:** A kite is a quadrilateral with two non-overlapping adjacent pairs of sides of equal length.
  - If labeled $ABCD$, we have $AB = BC$ and $CD = DA$.
- **Key Properties:**
  1. **Diagonals are perpendicular** to each other.
  2. **One diagonal bisects the other** (e.g., diagonal $BD$ bisects $AC$, so $AO = OC$).
  3. **One diagonal bisects the interior angles** it passes through.
  4. Opposite sides are *not* parallel, and all four sides are *not* equal (unless it is a rhombus).

## Slide 9 — Trapeziums and Isosceles Trapeziums
- **Trapezium Definition:** A trapezium is a quadrilateral with **at least one pair of parallel opposite sides**.
  - **Angle Property:** The consecutive interior angles on the non-parallel sides add up to $180^\circ$.
- **Isosceles Trapezium:** A special trapezium where the non-parallel sides have equal length.
  - **Properties:**
    1. The angles opposite to the equal sides are equal (e.g., base angles are equal: $\angle U = \angle V$).
    2. The other pair of opposite sides are not parallel, so it is *not* a parallelogram.

## Slide 10 — Understanding the Quadrilateral Venn Diagram
Let's see how these shapes relate to one another:
- **Parallelogram Family:** Rectangles, rhombuses, and squares are all parallelograms because they have two pairs of parallel opposite sides.
- **Rhombus & Rectangle Overlap:** A square is the unique shape that is both a rectangle (all angles $90^\circ$) and a rhombus (all sides equal).
- **Kite & Parallelogram Overlap:** A rhombus and a square are both kites because they have adjacent sides equal.
- **Trapeziums:** Since they require *at least* one pair of parallel sides, all parallelograms are technically trapeziums.

## Slide 11 — Worked Examples: Angle Calculations
- **Example 1: Finding angles in a parallelogram.**
  - Let parallelogram $ABCD$ have one angle $\angle A = 30^\circ$. Find the other three angles.
  - *Solution:*
    - In a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal. So, $\angle C = \angle A = 30^\circ$.
    - Adjacent angles are supplementary. So, $\angle B = 180^\circ - \angle A = 180^\circ - 30^\circ = 150^\circ$.
    - Since opposite angles are equal, $\angle D = \angle B = 150^\circ$.
    - The angles are: $30^\circ, 150^\circ, 30^\circ, 150^\circ$.

- **Example 2: Finding angles in a rhombus.**
  - Rhombus $ABCD$ has diagonal $AC$. If the angle $\angle DAB = 50^\circ$, find the four angles formed by the diagonal $AC$.
  - *Solution:*
    - The diagonals of a rhombus bisect its angles.
    - Thus, the diagonal $AC$ splits the angle $\angle DAB = 50^\circ$ into two equal angles of $25^\circ$.
    - Since opposite angles in a rhombus are equal, $\angle DCB = \angle DAB = 50^\circ$.
    - The diagonal $AC$ also splits $\angle DCB$ into two equal angles of $25^\circ$.

## Slide 12 — Worked Examples: Geometric Deductions
- **Example 3: Square within a square.**
  - If $CASE$ is a square and $U, V, W, X$ are the midpoints of its sides, what shape is $UVWX$?
  - *Solution:*
    - Let the side of square $CASE$ be $2x$. The half-sides are $x$.
    - Using the Pythagorean theorem in right-angled triangle $CUV$: $UV^2 = CU^2 + CV^2 = x^2 + x^2 = 2x^2$, so $UV = x\sqrt{2}$.
    - Similarly, all four sides of $UVWX$ are equal to $x\sqrt{2}$.
    - The angles at the corners of $UVWX$ can be proven to be $90^\circ$ because the base angles of the isosceles right triangles are $45^\circ$, and by linear pair, the angle of the inner shape is $180^\circ - 45^\circ - 45^\circ = 90^\circ$.
    - Therefore, $UVWX$ is also a **square**.

## Slide 13 — Common Mistakes to Avoid
- **Mistake 1: Assuming all trapeziums are parallelograms.**
  - *Incorrect:* "Since a trapezium has parallel sides, it is a parallelogram."
  - *Correct:* A trapezium only needs *one* pair of parallel sides. A parallelogram needs *two* pairs of parallel sides.
- **Mistake 2: Thinking diagonals of a parallelogram are equal.**
  - *Incorrect:* "In parallelogram $ABCD$, the diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ are of the same length."
  - *Correct:* Diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, but they are generally of different lengths unless the parallelogram is a rectangle or square.
- **Mistake 3: Thinking any quadrilateral with perpendicular diagonals is a rhombus.**
  - *Incorrect:* "If the diagonals of a quadrilateral intersect at $90^\circ$, it must be a rhombus."
  - *Correct:* Kites also have perpendicular diagonals, but their sides are not all equal.

## Slide 14 — Lesson Summary
Let's review our quadrilateral toolkit:
- **Angle Sum:** Sum of all interior angles of any quadrilateral is $360^\circ$.
- **Rectangle:** All angles $90^\circ$, opposite sides equal and parallel, equal diagonals that bisect each other.
- **Square:** Rectangle with equal sides, diagonals bisect each other at $90^\circ$ and bisect the corner angles.
- **Parallelogram:** Two pairs of parallel opposite sides, opposite sides equal, opposite angles equal, adjacent angles supplementary, diagonals bisect each other.
- **Rhombus:** Parallelogram with equal sides, diagonals bisect each other at $90^\circ$ and bisect the angles.
- **Kite:** Adjacent sides equal, perpendicular diagonals.
- **Trapezium:** At least one pair of parallel sides.
